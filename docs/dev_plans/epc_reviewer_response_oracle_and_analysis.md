# ePC reviewer response: linear oracle, backprop-regime documentation, analysis script

## Context

The reviewer replied to the eight observation bullets that accompanied the resnet18 ePC-vs-sPC convergence figure (`examples/epc_spc_resnet18_compare.py --mode convergence`). Their requests split three ways:

- **Test-suite invariants.** A linear oracle that returns the exact equilibrium state and energy for a range of linear networks, with both `EPCInference` and the state-based solvers checked against it, plus a test of the oracle itself (hard-coded numbers on tiny models, or code readable enough to be obviously right via Innocenti et al. 2024, Theorem 1). A warning that 1-step ePC is backprop with gradients scaled by `eta_infer`.
- **Analytical questions.** Why sPC struggles with deep layers (bullet 1); what sets the equilibrium energy spacing across layers (bullets 2, 3); ePC stability in deep networks and how to pick the largest stable `eta_infer` (bullet 4); the training collapses and whether they occur under muPC (bullet 6, confirmed by the user: the resnet18 demo is a muPC model); how many steps sPC needs to reach equilibrium and why oracle checks should stay at 5 layers or fewer (bullet 7).
- **Warning adequacy.** The user asked whether the existing caution against `infer_steps=1` says the right thing and appears where users will see it.

Facts established 2026-09-03, revised 2026-09-04 after review of this plan against the sweep data:

1. The ePC paper (Goemaere et al., arXiv 2505.20137, Appendix C.3, Theorem C.9) states two backprop-equivalence conditions: `infer_steps = 1` gives backprop gradients scaled by the error learning rate, and `eta × steps ≪ 1` gives backprop gradients scaled by `eta × steps`. The paper kept its own `eta × steps` above that threshold deliberately. Its Case 2 approximation holds while the error-perturbed output prediction stays close to the feedforward one, so the stated condition carries an implicit O(1) Jacobian scale; the graph-dependent form is η·T·λ_max(H_ε) ≪ 1 (fact 5).
2. Regime data. In the 2-epoch sweep (tables at the end of `docs/dev_plans_archive/epc_inference_solver.md`) accuracy departs from the small-η·T limit (38.8%) at η·T ≈ 0.01–0.02 for every η tested (1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2) and reaches the PC plateau (≈ 31%) by η·T ≈ 0.15; at η·T = 0.05 (eta 1e-2, 5 steps) it is 35.0%, halfway. Reading the accuracy interpolation as the relaxed fraction of the error modes that matter puts the effective excited eigenvalue of the muPC resnet18's ε-Hessian at order 10¹–10², far above the precision floor of 1; section D.5 measures it. The current default `EPCInference(eta_infer=1e-3, infer_steps=5)` (η·T = 0.005) sits at the backprop value at 2 epochs (38.70% against 38.84% at T = 1), and the 100-epoch demo at `--infer_steps 1` (76.73%) matches the backprop trainer on the same graph (77.11%).
3. Collapse data. The six 100-epoch logs in the project root (`sweep_eta{0.001,0.01}_steps{1,2,5}.log`): (1e-3, 1) 76.73%; (1e-3, 2) 75.76%; (1e-3, 5), the library default, 54.76% at epoch 10 and 9.68% at epoch 20; (1e-2, T) at chance by epoch 10 for T ∈ {1, 2, 5}. Only η·T ≤ 0.002 survived 100 epochs, while at 2 epochs η·T = 0.16 still trains, so collapse is a training-horizon effect: the weights grow, λ_max(H_ε) grows with the downstream weight products, and a fixed η eventually exceeds the stability bound 2/λ_max. T = 1 is not exempt. One step from ε = 0 puts each error mode at ε_1 = η·λ·ε*_λ, so a mode with η·λ > 1 overshoots its equilibrium and with η·λ > 2 lands farther from it than the start. The (1e-1, 1) collapse at 2 epochs and the (1e-2, 1) collapse by epoch 10 are that mechanism at T = 1.
4. The existing caution ("Use infer_steps > 1") keys on the wrong criterion, and the tuning table in the inference guide recommends `infer_steps` 1 to 5 for ePC two paragraphs below it.
5. A design review of the oracle math verified the quadratic form, the ε-coordinate Hessian, and the exactness of the 1-step ε identity, and corrected three claims: an unclamped source node contributes no curvature floor in ε coordinates; ePC's stability bound shrinks as downstream weight products grow, so the "ePC is better conditioned" claim must be stated per regime; and the 1-step weight gradients equal η × backprop only to first order in η·λ_max, because `finalize_state` re-derives the latents before the local weight gradient is taken (the paper's Case 1 proof evaluates ∂ŝ/∂θ at the unperturbed point).

User decisions (2026-09-03): keep the defaults and document the regime at every touch point; no `warnings.warn`, the demos print the regime instead; the oracle lives in a package module shared by tests and the analysis script.

User decisions (2026-09-04, after review): keep `EPCInference(eta_infer=1e-3, infer_steps=5)` with a docstring that states the measured 100-epoch outcome, and let λ_max tracking on resnet18 decide an adaptive-rate follow-up; test the collapse hypothesis by tracking λ_max during resnet18 training behind the GPU flag, not on a CPU MNIST MLP; `regime_label` is graph-aware and takes λ_max; this plan is self-contained and builds the weight-gradient parity test (the mean-gradient-normalization plan lives on another branch with no ePC scope and ships first).

## Symbols

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| z_t | latent of node t, one row per sample, `NodeState.z_latent` |
| μ_t | node t's prediction of z_t from its in-edge sources, `NodeState.z_mu` |
| ε_t | prediction error z_t − μ_t, `NodeState.error`; the relaxed variable under `EPCInference` |
| W_eff[s→t] | effective edge matrix: the muPC `forward_scale` for the edge (1.0 when absent) times the Linear weight for that edge key (or times the IdentityNode `scale` times I) |
| E | total energy, Σ over in_degree > 0 nodes of ½·precision·‖z_t − μ_t‖², the set the trainer and both solvers sum |
| A, c | the quadratic form of E over the stacked free latents on a linear-Gaussian graph, E = ½‖A z_free − c‖² |
| B, M | B: strictly block-lower-triangular map z_free ← z_free through the edges; M = (I − B)⁻¹, the ε → z map |
| H_z, H_ε | Hessians of E in latent coordinates (AᵀA) and error coordinates (MᵀAᵀAM) |
| S | I + Σ_l P_lᵀP_l on a chain, P_l = W_eff[l+1]⋯W_eff[L] the map from ε_l to the output prediction; Innocenti's Theorem 1 matrix |
| λ, λ_max | eigenvalues of H_ε and its top eigenvalue; from `eigvalsh` on linear graphs (oracle) or `top_epsilon_eigenvalue` on any graph (power iteration) |
| g | the backprop activation gradient ∂L/∂z_t at the feedforward point, L the clamped output's energy; equals ∇_ε E at ε = 0 |
| f_max, f_min | relaxed fraction of the fastest and slowest error modes after T steps: 1 − (1 − η·λ_max)^T and 1 − (1 − η·p_min)^T |
| η, T | `eta_infer`, `infer_steps` |

## Deliverables

### A. `fabricpc/utils/linear_pc_oracle.py` (new, ~260 lines)

Two parts, separated in the file and its docstring: the exact-equilibrium core (assembly and solve, pure NumPy float64, ~80 lines, the part the reviewer must find obviously right) and the spectral diagnostics (NumPy, plus one JAX power iteration).

Core: the definition of the linear-Gaussian energy as a quadratic, solved exactly. Reads only params, edges, `node_info.scaling_config.forward_scale`, the IdentityNode `scale` in `node_config`, biases, and precision (`node_info.energy.config["precision"]`, a read-only mapping, default 1.0). Never calls node code or solver code.

Conventions, asserted in code: nodes enumerated in `structure.node_order` (topological on a DAG), so B is strictly block-lower-triangular; samples are columns of c; weights act in row convention (z_mu = z_s @ W, one matrix per in-edge summed), so the block acting on a stacked column is the transpose. Params are keyed by edge key `"{source}->{target}:{slot}"`, biases by `"b"` with shape `(1, d_t)`.

Assembly, for each node t with in_degree > 0 (one residual row block, precision p_t):
- block(t, t) = √p_t·I if t is unclamped; otherwise the clamp moves to c.
- block(t, s) = −√p_t·W_eff[s→t]ᵀ for each unclamped source s; clamped sources move to c.
- c_t = √p_t·(b_t + Σ_{clamped s} W_eff[s→t]ᵀ z_s − [t clamped]·z_t).
- Unclamped sources (in_degree 0) get columns but no row: they have no energy term. Under ePC their `z_mu` is fixed for the segment at the value `begin_segment` assigns (the initializer's latent draw; the raw initial state holds zeros), which affects ε* but not z* or E*.
- W_eff for a `Linear` target: `forward_scale[edge_key]` (1.0 when the key is absent) times `params.nodes[t].weights[edge_key]`. For an `IdentityNode` target with in_degree > 0: `forward_scale` times `node_config["scale"]` times I. A source `IdentityNode` never runs `predict`, so its scale is inert and the oracle ignores it.

API:
- `validate_linear_gaussian(structure)`: DAG by back-edge check, every edge s→t must satisfy `node_order.index(s) < node_order.index(t)` (`len(schedule) == len(node_order)` is not a DAG test: at `unroll=1` a cycle's members are visited once, pinned by `tests/test_topological_schedule.py:121-124`; `structure.config["unroll"]` is stored for DAGs too); node class `Linear` or `IdentityNode`, `IdentityActivation`, `GaussianEnergy`, `flatten_input=False`, rank-1 node shapes, no `energy()` override. Raises `ValueError` naming the node or edge.
- `effective_edge_matrices(params, structure)`, `assemble_linear_quadratic(params, structure, clamps, *, source_means=None) -> LinearQuadratic` (fields: `free`, `rows`, offsets, `A`, `c`, `B_lower`, `M`, `z_ff`).
- `linear_equilibrium(params, structure, clamps, *, source_means=None) -> LinearEquilibrium`: `z_star`, `z_mu_star`, `error_star`, per-node `node_energy` (batch,), `total_energy` (batch,), `min_singular_value`. Uses `np.linalg.lstsq`; raises if rank < D (non-unique equilibrium, usually an unclamped source whose outgoing maps are not jointly injective).
- `theorem1_energy(params, structure, clamps) -> (E_star, S, r)`: chain-only closed form E* = ½·Σ_n r_n S⁻¹ r_nᵀ, r = y − μ_out at the feedforward point. Biases and muPC scales enter through r and P_l.
- Diagnostics: `latent_hessian(quad)`, `epsilon_hessian(quad)`, `stability_bound(H) = 2/λ_max`, `excited_eigenvalues(H, g0)` (eigenvalues whose eigenvectors have non-negligible overlap with the initial gradient; from ε = 0 with uniform precision and no unclamped sources these are eig(S)), `relaxed_fraction(eta, steps, eigs) = 1 − (1 − eta·eigs)^steps`, `steps_to_contract(eta, eigs, ratio)`, `flatten_free`/`unflatten_free` in `node_order` (never `tree_leaves`, which sorts dict keys).
- `top_epsilon_eigenvalue(params, state, clamps, structure, iters=30, key=None) -> float`: power iteration on the Hessian-vector product through `EPCInference.error_energy` (B.1), `jax.jvp(jax.grad(...))`, jitted, pytree axpy via `tree_map`. Works on any graph the solver accepts, nonlinear included. The demos, the compare script, and the analysis script call it on the first batch at init; the tests validate it against `eigvalsh(epsilon_hessian(quad))`.

Module docstring states the regime-dependent conditioning result, not a blanket claim: λ_min(H_z) decays with depth even for benign weights (sPC's slow mode); λ_max(H_ε) = 1 + σ_max(J)² grows with downstream weight products (ePC's shrinking stability bound); from ε = 0 with uniform precision and no unclamped sources, ePC's trajectory lives in the d_y-dimensional row space of J and sees only eig(S). It then states the regime parameter: each excited mode relaxes by 1 − (1 − ηλ)^T per T steps; backprop-like requires η·T·λ_max ≪ 1; near equilibrium requires η·T·λ_min,excited ≳ 3; stability requires η·λ_max < 2, T = 1 included as one-step overshoot (ε_1 = η·λ·ε*_λ).

### B. `fabricpc/core/inference_epc.py`

1. Extract the `energy_of` closure (lines 142–153) verbatim into `@classmethod error_energy(cls, params, state, clamps, structure) -> (energy_of, errors)`; `forward_value_and_grad` calls it. Same ops, same order, bit-identical gradients (pinned by the existing `TestGradientCorrectness`). Purpose: Hessian-vector products `jax.jvp(jax.grad(lambda e: energy_of(e)[0]), (errors,), (v,))` for `top_epsilon_eigenvalue`, the analysis script, and one new test.
2. `regime_label(self, lambda_max: float) -> str`: requires the measured or oracle λ_max; no overload without it (an η·T-only label reads the slowest mode and mislabels the resnet18 sweep by about 10×, see Alternatives). Computes f_max = 1 − (1 − η·λ_max)^T and f_min = 1 − (1 − η)^T. Returns `"unstable: eta*lambda_max = 3.2 > 2"` when η·λ_max > 2; otherwise e.g. `"eta*T*lambda_max = 0.38 (fastest error mode relaxed 32%, slowest 0.5%): partially relaxed"`. Bands on f_max: below 0.1 "backprop-like", 0.1 to 0.9 "partially relaxed", above 0.9 "near PC equilibrium". One owner of the wording. The demos print it with "at init", since λ_max grows during training.
3. Class docstring: replace the `infer_steps` sentence with the mechanism. One step from ε = 0 leaves ε_t = −η·g_t exactly (the backprop activation gradient). The local weight gradients, taken at the re-derived latents, then match backprop's to first order in η·λ_max(H_ε): hidden layers scaled by η, the output layer unscaled (paper Theorem C.9 Case 1; the derive-then-gradient order makes the weight-gradient identity first-order while the ε identity is exact). After T steps each excited error mode relaxes by 1 − (1 − ηλ)^T: backprop-like when η·T·λ_max ≪ 1, near equilibrium when η·T·λ_min,excited ≳ 3, stable when η·λ_max < 2, T = 1 included as one-step overshoot. Measured on the muPC resnet18 demo: the 2-epoch sweep implies an effective excited eigenvalue of order 10¹–10² at init (`scripts/epc_analysis.py --section stability --resnet18` measures it); the defaults (η·T = 0.005) train as backprop for tens of epochs and collapsed at epoch 20 of a 100-epoch run while T ∈ {1, 2} survived, consistent with λ_max growing past 2/η as the weights grow. The defaults are kept pending a stability-aware rate; `top_epsilon_eigenvalue` gives the bound for any graph at any point in training. Under Adam the η scaling of hidden-layer gradients is normalized away, so 1-step ePC with Adam trains as backprop with Adam. Keep the signature defaults unchanged (`tests/test_doc_defaults.py` pins them against the guide's Default column; it does not parse the tuning table).

## Alternatives considered

- **Runtime warning at construction.** Keyed on `infer_steps == 1`: fires on the wrong criterion (T = 5 at η 1e-3 is equally backprop). Keyed on η·T: fires on the library's own defaults and ignores λ_max. Rejected in favor of docs plus printed regime labels (user decision).

Rejected; `regime_label(lambda_max)` with the measured top eigenvalue chosen (user decision 2026-09-04).
- **Change the defaults.** (1e-3, 2) survived 100 epochs; (1e-4, 5) sits below the observed collapse boundary but was never run at 100 epochs. Any fixed η collapses once λ_max grows past 2/η, so a new constant only moves the horizon. Rejected (user decision 2026-09-04): keep the defaults, document the measured outcome, let the λ_max tracking motivate an adaptive rate.
- **CPU MNIST collapse experiment.** The collapses occurred on the muPC resnet18 between epochs 10 and 20 of 100; a few CPU epochs of a 4-layer MLP cannot reproduce them and the outcome would be uninformative. Rejected; λ_max tracking on the demo graph behind a GPU flag chosen (user decision 2026-09-04), with the CPU section showing the mechanism exactly via the oracle.
- **Deferring the weight-gradient parity test to the mean-gradient-normalization plan.** That plan is on another branch with no ePC scope. Rejected (user decision 2026-09-04); the parity test runs here at batch 1, independent of the normalization change.
- **Oracle via `jax.jacfwd` of an affine residual built from the nodes' own `predict`.** Shorter, but not independent of the code under test. Rejected for explicit block assembly from params.
- **Oracle under `tests/` only.** The analysis script needs the same code; duplication or a `sys.path` import. Rejected (user decision).
- **Compare solvers only against each other (existing `TestSPCEquivalence`).** Two wrong solvers can agree; the oracle pins the exact answer and the gradient scale. The existing equivalence tests stay for nonlinear coverage.
- **Stability by trial and error only.** For linear graphs the bound is exact (2/λ_max(H_ε)); for any graph power iteration on the HVP gives the local bound at a few forward-backward passes. Chosen over trial and error.
